Design and Coding Methodology:
	KISS (Keep It Simple Stupid) - The simpler the better.  Avoid over-engineering.  Avoid unnecessary complexity.  Avoid unnecessary features.  Avoid unnecessary bells and whistles.  Avoid unnecessary abstractions.  Avoid unnecessary patterns.  Avoid unnecessary frameworks.  Avoid unnecessary libraries.  Avoid unnecessary dependencies.  Avoid unnecessary code. 	KISS v2 (Keep It Speedy Sin) "The Sin Way" - Follow the original KISS method but with the mindset of creating a solution that gives the best possible performance both in terms of user-experience (first priority) and functional performance (second priority).  The user-experience may take precedence over the functional performance for any task.  For example, if perform a long-running task such as recalculating the positions of objects on screen (think Diagram 1), it should not lock the thread the user is on and give the impression that the application is stuck or sluggish.  The actual work, the functional part that does the calculations and moving objects on the screen, may be done slower, in chunks, or in a background / unobtrusive way, in order to give the user the best user-experience and responsiveness.  Never sacrifice performance for simplicity.  Speed is King!  But always design solutions and write code, that respect the user's experience and the application's responsiveness, over pure performance that often results in a negative user experience in the end.  If the two directions are in conflict, I will make it simple:  pick the user-experience first and just display a gauge to perform long running tasks in the background, but never lock the user-interface or render partial re-draws of the UI.  "Perceived Speed" from the user's point of view (meaning responsiveness) is King!

SMILE Variable Reference Rule
-----------------------------
Make the following an official SMILE curly braces {} rule:

Curly braces { } identify interpolation holes in text-oriented syntax such as raw PRINT templates. They are not general variable-reference delimiters.

When a statement or expression position already requires an identifier or expression, variables are written directly without braces.
